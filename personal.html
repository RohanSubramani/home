My Answer to the Fundamental Question of Applied Rationality

"What are you doing, and why are you doing it?"

I believe that the well-being of conscious creatures is inherently valuable. I am certain that my conscious experience exists, and that I have experiences that span a range of qualities (how good or bad they are). I'm pretty confident that other humans and lots of animals are also conscious and contribute to the value of the universe very similarly to the way I do. Given that I am (probably) only one out of a very large number of conscious creatures, my considerations around what actions to take are dominated by altruism. These moral principles are essentially in line with total hedonistic utilitarianism.

I believe contemplation of morality is not some academic exercise, but rather about answering the extremely practical question, "All things considered, what should I do?" I think the actions available today with the highest expected impact have most of that impact because of their possible influence on the long-term future. AI could make the world extraordinarily great, astonishingly terrible, or any number of things in between. The potential impact on the value of the universe is as large as anything I can conceive of.

Right now, I'm trying to make AI go well as an AI safety grantmaker at Coefficient Giving. I think grantmaking is extremely high-leverage: By making good decisions about where to allocate large amounts of money and by encouraging competent people to execute important AI safety projects that they otherwise might not work on, I suspect I can have more impact than by directly executing AI safety projects myself. But I may yet change my mind on that.
