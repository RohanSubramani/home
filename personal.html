My (Partial, Abbreviated) Answer to the Fundamental Question of Applied Rationality

"What are you doing, and why are you doing it?"

I believe that the well-being of conscious creatures is inherently valuable. I am ~certain that my conscious experience exists, and that I have experiences that span a range of qualities (how good or bad they are). I'm pretty confident that other humans and lots of animals are also conscious and contribute to the value of the universe very similarly to the way I do. Given that I am (probably) only one out of a very large number of conscious creatures, my considerations around what actions to take are dominated by altruism. These moral principles are essentially in line with total hedonistic utilitarianism.

I believe contemplation of morality is not some academic exercise, but rather about answering the extremely practical question, "All things considered, what should I do?" I think the actions available today with the highest expected impact have most of that impact because of their possible influence on the long-term future. AI could make the world extraordinarily great, astonishingly terrible, or any number of things in between. The potential impact on the value of the universe is as large as anything I can conceive of. The fact that I expect AI will probably dominate top experts at virtually all cognitive tasks within a decade makes this especially urgent and obvious as the top priority.

Right now, I'm trying to make AI go well as an AI safety grantmaker at Coefficient Giving. I think grantmaking is extremely high-leverage: By making good decisions about where to allocate large amounts of money and by encouraging competent people to execute important AI safety projects that they otherwise might not work on, I suspect I can have more impact than by directly executing AI safety projects myself. But I may yet change my mind on that.

Some other parts of my answer to the fundamental question:

  • I'm vegan because I believe that factory farming involves immense suffering for animals—possibly enough to outweigh all happiness experienced by all humans—and I don't want to contribute to that. (Realistically I think individual veganism isn't the most impactful thing one can do to reduce animal suffering: there's more good to be done by donating to the most effective animal welfare charities and maybe by trying to develop alternative proteins that allow us to obsolete factory farming. But I still think being vegan is much better than not being vegan.)
  • I donate a nontrivial fraction of my income to high-impact opportunities, including US political campaigns for candidates who seem to really understand and care about AI x-risk, other AI safety organizations, and the most cost-effective animal welfare and global health charities. I was donating 10% for the past few years, and I plan to donate a higher percentage now that I'm making more money. I think it's extremely clear that much of my money can do much more good for others than it can for me, so this feels like a really easy choice. I'm also quite happy to spend money on making my life more comfortable and productive in ways that actually have a functional difference, especially because I think my work is more impactful than my individual donations. (This is especially obvious now that my grantmaking work involves making donations at a much larger scale than my own personal budget would allow.)
  • I run a weekly writing group, because I think writing and thinking are almost synonymous and I want to do good thinking.
